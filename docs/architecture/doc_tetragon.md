# Athanor runtime events (Tetragon)

Status: **revision 1, draft for maintainer review (2026-10-06).** It is the specification that maintainer decision A2-10b (#153) asks for: Tetragon does real work and is not deleted. It designs the tracing policies the image ships, how the service is enabled and confined, how an event reaches the user as a notice and as a row of the shield's sheet, the limits on volume and on what is kept, the acceptance gate and the order of construction. Every rule marked _(Proposal)_ awaits the maintainer; section 6 lists the questions.

## 2. Decisions

**TG1. What Tetragon is for in Athanor.** A local detector of four behaviours that the confinement of tier 2 and the image's protections of tier 3 are meant to make impossible or rare. It tells the user, on this machine only, when one happens. It is not an enforcement layer at 1.0, not a substitute for TM3's `ReadOnlyPaths=` or for Flatpak's sandbox, and not a source of telemetry.

- Every shipped policy observes and never kills (`Post` only, no `Sigkill`, no `Override`). Enforcement is open question Q1.
- Events never leave the machine (TG9). Nothing in this document moves the shield's badge (TG8).

**TG2. Where the policies and the daemon's settings live.**

- The policies are files of the package, under `/usr/lib/tetragon/tetragon.tp.d/`, one per behaviour, except persistence, which takes two files to respect upstream's selector limits (TG3): `athanor-persistence-create.yaml`, `athanor-persistence-write.yaml`, `athanor-exec-temporary.yaml`, `athanor-credentials.yaml`, `athanor-modules.yaml`. Each `metadata.name` begins with `athanor-`, which is how the export filter (TG7) and the relay (TG7) recognise them. Being under `/usr`, they are part of the verified image and change only with it.
- The daemon's settings are drop-ins of the package, one file per option, under `/usr/lib/tetragon/tetragon.conf.d/`, among them `tracing-policy-dir` set to `/usr/lib/tetragon/tetragon.tp.d`. `/etc/tetragon/` stays the administrator's, as upstream reserves it: a file there overrides ours, and an administrator who wants their own policies sets `tracing-policy-dir` in `/etc/tetragon/tetragon.conf.d/`. The package stops shipping `/etc/tetragon/tetragon.yaml` and keeps the empty `/etc/tetragon/tetragon.tp.d` only if Q6 says so.
- **The BPF objects follow the image.** The drop-ins set `bpf-lib` to `/usr/lib/tetragon/`, and the package installs the objects at `/usr/lib/tetragon/bpf` (the spec's `%{_sharedstatedir}/tetragon/bpf` becomes `%{_prefix}/lib/tetragon/bpf`). On ostree and bootc `/usr` is replaced with the image and `/var` is not, so objects left in `/var/lib` can outlive the binary they were built for, and after an update the daemon would load stale objects or refuse to start. The package ships a `tmpfiles.d` line `R! /var/lib/tetragon/bpf` to remove the copies that earlier packages left. State that must persist lives in two places only: `/var/log/tetragon` (the export file and its rotated backups, TG7) and `/var/lib/tetragon` (`StateDirectory=`, TG10). The pinned maps under `/sys/fs/bpf/tetragon` are rebuilt at every start and persist nothing. That the daemon writes nothing under `bpf-lib`, which is read-only under `ProtectSystem=strict`, is part of S1.
- The unit passes no option on its command line (`ExecStart=/usr/bin/tetragon`), so the drop-ins are the one place the settings are read from. The current `--config-dir=/etc/tetragon/tetragon.tp.d` and `--export-filename=` leave.

**TG3. Policy: writes to the persistence paths by a process outside the host's mount namespace.**

- **Reason.** TM2 promises that tier 2 cannot write the persistence paths of TM3; TM4 states where that promise leaks (a Flatpak with home access). This policy reports the leak when it is used, and reports a regression of SD8's `ReadOnlyPaths=` if one ships.
- **Tier.** TM2, and the leaks of TM4.
- **Hooks and the selector limits.** Upstream bounds a hook at 5 selectors, a `matchArgs` at 4 values (8 for a `file` argument) and a postfix at 128 characters (section 1), so one policy cannot carry an open-ended list. TG3 is two policies, each sized to the limits _(Proposal)_.
  - `athanor-persistence-create.yaml`: creation of an entry in a persistence directory, seen on the parent directory through the path-based LSM hooks, which exist because `CONFIG_SECURITY_PATH=y`: `security_path_mknod` (reached from `open` with `O_CREAT` on a new file), `security_path_mkdir`, `security_path_symlink`, `security_path_link` and `security_path_rename` (the new directory). Their `path` argument is the parent directory, matched with `Postfix` against the directory part of each TM3 row (`/.config/systemd/user`, `/.config/autostart`, `/.config/environment.d`, `/.local/share/applications`, and so on). A selector carries 4 directories and a hook 5 selectors: 20 directories per hook. The four directories named today fit in one selector.
  - `athanor-persistence-write.yaml`: a write to an existing start-up file, seen by `security_file_permission` with the write mask, its `file` argument matched with `Postfix` against `/.bashrc`, `/.bash_profile`, `/.bash_login` and `/.profile`: one selector, with room for four more.
  - **The list is bounded and a test enforces it.** A row added to TM3 adds a value here in the same change while there is room; the 21st directory or the 9th start-up file needs a further policy file. Step 2 adds a unit test that parses each shipped policy and fails when a hook has more than 5 selectors, a `matchArgs` more than 4 values (8 for `file`) or a postfix is longer than 128 characters, so a row that does not fit fails in CI and not at load time on a machine. The list follows TM3's table at the time of step 2, including the rows TM3 still marks as proposals.
  - `security_file_permission` fires on every read and write of the machine. The kernel-side prefilter below (`Mnt` `NotIn` `host_ns`) keeps most of them out of the export; the cost of the hook itself is spike S2.
- **Prefilter in the kernel.** `matchNamespaces` with `Mnt` `NotIn` `host_ns`. A process of the broker's `confined` class and every Flatpak application runs in its own mount namespace; the user's shell, an `unconfined` application and the user manager do not. Without this filter every save of a desktop entry by the user would be an event.
- **Classification in the relay** (TG7). A process whose cgroup is `app-flatpak-*.scope` or `app-athanor-*@*.service` (SD8) is tier 2 and raises a notice. A process in a container (toolbox, rootless podman) is tier 1 by TM5 and is recorded only. One of our own hardened units is recorded only. A process that has exited before the relay reads its cgroup raises a notice: the cautious default. How the relay tells a toolbox from a Flatpak when `/proc/<pid>` is gone is spike S3.
- **Gaps, stated.** A write to an existing file in a persistence directory (for example an autostart entry rewritten in place) is seen only if the file itself is named in `athanor-persistence-write`: `Postfix` matches the end of a path, so a directory cannot be matched for the files inside it. A tier 2 process that persuades a tier 1 process to write for it is not seen.
- **Expected noise.** Low. The skeleton files are written once, at account creation, by a host-namespace process. Our own units with a private mount namespace do not write these paths (SD17: `ProtectHome=read-only`).

**TG4. Policy: a program executed from a temporary directory.**

- **Reason.** Dropping a binary in `/tmp` or `/dev/shm` and running it is the common second stage of an exploit chain and of downloaded malware. On Athanor both directories are writable by every user and mounted without `noexec`.
- **Tier.** TM2 (a confined application that escapes far enough to exec on the host), and TM1 as information to the user.
- **Hook.** `security_bprm_check` with its `linux_binprm` argument (upstream example [security_bprm_check.yaml](https://github.com/cilium/tetragon/blob/v1.7.1/examples/tracingpolicy/security_bprm_check.yaml)). First selector: `Prefix` `/tmp/`, `/dev/shm/`, `/var/tmp/` and `/run/user/` (four values, the limit of a `matchArgs`; the last two are Q3), with `matchNamespaces` `Mnt` `In` `host_ns`, action `Post` with `rateLimit: "1m"` and `rateLimitScope: "global"` (TG8). A second selector with the same filter and action is held for executing a `memfd` (spike S4); the hook has room for five.
- **What it misses, stated.** An interpreter given a file as an argument (`bash /tmp/x`, `python3 /tmp/x.py`) executes the interpreter, not the file; a script run directly through its `#!` line is seen.
- **Expected noise, and what is filtered or shown.** Medium for developers, near zero otherwise. Three places decide, in this order _(Proposal)_.
  - **Filtered in the kernel: anything that is not the host's `/tmp`.** The selector's `Mnt` `In` `host_ns` leaves out every process in its own mount namespace: Flatpak applications (a Flatpak's `/tmp` is private and is not the host's), bwrap, rootless podman and toolbox (tier 1 by TM5, and a container), and our own units with `PrivateTmp=`. What they execute from their own `/tmp` cannot be reached from the host and is not seen at all. A confined application that escapes far enough to execute from the host's `/tmp` is in the host mount namespace and is seen.
  - **Recorded, never a notice: known developer and packaging patterns, classified in the relay.** Binaries under `/tmp/.mount_*/` (an AppImage's mount: the user ran an AppImage) and under `/tmp/go-build*/` (`go run` and `go test`). Electron applications and Steam are not known to execute from these prefixes on the host; none is classified until spike S8 finds one, and it then joins this list by a change to the relay and not by a new policy. rpm and dnf use `/var/tmp/rpm-tmp.*` as an argument of `sh`, which is the interpreter case below and not an exec of the file (S8 confirms on `rpm-ostree` and `nix-daemon`).
  - **Shown: every other exec from the four prefixes.** A binary dropped straight in `/tmp`, `/dev/shm`, `/var/tmp` or `/run/user/<uid>/`, or a script run through its `#!` line. The relay suppresses the notice for this category while developer mode is on (`doc_software.md` SW2), records the events all the same, and aggregates them (TG8). The acceptance gate runs with developer mode off.

**TG5. Policy: credential changes towards root.**

- **Reason.** A local privilege escalation ends with a process holding uid 0 or new capabilities it did not have at exec.
- **Tier.** TM7. Tetragon does not defend tier 3, which belongs to `doc_kernel_profile.md`; it gives the user a record of an escalation that the other layers did not stop.
- **Hooks** _(Proposal)._ Three parts. Each carries kernel-side filters that keep root and namespaced callers out of the export.
  - The `setuid` family to root, from upstream's [privileges-raise.yaml](https://github.com/cilium/tetragon/blob/v1.7.1/examples/policylibrary/privileges/privileges-raise.yaml): `__sys_setuid`, `__sys_setreuid`, `__sys_setresuid`, `__sys_setfsuid` and the `gid` counterparts with an argument `Equal` `0`, each `Post`, with `matchCapabilities` `Effective` `NotIn` `CAP_SETUID` (`CAP_SETGID` for the `gid` calls). A caller that holds the capability is root, a setuid-root program or inside a user namespace, and is not reported; what remains is a process without the right to change its uid asking for uid 0. The hook fires on the call, so what it reports is an attempt, and the notice says so (TG8). That the capability check sees the caller's own effective set at this hook is part of spike S8.
  - `security_capset`. Upstream's rule posts every call whose new effective, inheritable or permitted set is non-zero (the same file, `NotEqual` `0` on arguments 2, 3 and 4); it is not limited to a gain, and it fires for every lowering of capabilities by a root daemon and every `capset` of a user-namespace sandbox. Ours posts the same calls with `matchNamespaces` `User` `In` `host_ns`, so processes inside a user namespace (rootless podman, Flatpak, bwrap, `unshare -r`) are not seen. The relay then keeps only a gain: a new effective set holding a capability that the process's capabilities at exec (`enable-process-cred`) did not hold.
  - A capability gained without an exec, through `matchCapabilityChanges` (`Effective`, `In`, `CAP_SYS_ADMIN`, `CAP_NET_RAW`) on a frequent hook such as `fd_install`, from upstream's [fd_install_cap_changes.yaml](https://github.com/cilium/tetragon/blob/v1.7.1/examples/tracingpolicy/fd_install_cap_changes.yaml), with two deviations. It sets `isNamespaceCapability: false` where upstream sets `true`, and it adds `matchNamespaces` `User` `In` `host_ns`. In `kernel.go`, `true` matches only while the process is outside the host user namespace, which is the opposite of what is wanted here: a gain inside a user namespace is normal (podman, Flatpak, bwrap) and is not reported. `false` ignores the user namespace, so the `host_ns` filter is what excludes it. This is the one rule that sees a kernel exploit that sets its own credentials, since that path makes no `setuid` call. Its cost on a busy hook is spike S5.
  - A raw hook on `commit_creds` is rejected: upstream notes it fires on every `execve` ([process-creds-changed.yaml](https://github.com/cilium/tetragon/blob/v1.7.1/examples/tracingpolicy/process-credentials/process-creds-changed.yaml)). Upstream's `create_user_ns` rule is left out: Flatpak and the browsers create a user namespace on every launch.
- **Classification in the relay.** `sudo`, `su`, `pkexec` and root daemons are what remains after the kernel filters on the `capset` and `fd_install` parts. The relay drops an event whose process had real uid 0 at exec (a root daemon), and one whose real uid was not 0 but effective uid was (`sudo`, `su`, `pkexec`) only when its binary is on an allow-list of the setuid-root programs the image ships, generated from the image at build time (step 3). A setuid-root binary that is not on the list raises a notice. The uids come from `enable-process-cred`.
- **Expected noise.** Not claimed to be none. The kernel filters and the relay remove the known sources (root daemons, `sudo`, user-namespace sandboxes); what remains on a healthy machine is measured, not asserted, in spike S8 and in the noise budget (step 6).

**TG6. Policy: kernel module loads.**

- **Reason.** With `MODULE_SIG_FORCE=y` an unsigned module is refused anyway. What remains useful is a record of which signed modules were loaded and of loads an unprivileged user triggered through autoloading (a socket family or a filesystem type nobody uses), a known way to reach rarely used kernel code.
- **Tier.** TM7.
- **Hooks.** As upstream's [monitor-kernel-modules.yaml](https://github.com/cilium/tetragon/blob/v1.7.1/examples/tracingpolicy/host-changes/monitor-kernel-modules.yaml) and [modules.yaml](https://github.com/cilium/tetragon/blob/v1.7.1/examples/policylibrary/modules.yaml): `security_kernel_module_request` (the requested name, with its return value), `security_kernel_read_file` with its second argument `Equal` `2` (reading a module), `do_init_module` (the module's name).
- **What is shown** _(Proposal)._ Recorded and counted, no notice, except for a module request whose process has a uid other than 0, which raises a notice. Q4. Each hook posts with `rateLimit: "1m"` and `rateLimitScope: "global"` (TG8).
- **Expected noise.** Tens of loads at boot, before any session; a few at hotplug.

**TG7. The event path: the export file and a system relay.**

- **The choice.** Tetragon writes its JSON export file; a small system service, `athanor-runtime-events`, reads it and serves each user their own events over the system bus; `athanor-sessiond` turns them into notices. Neither sessiond nor the bar reads Tetragon directly.
- **Why not the gRPC socket for the consumer.** The socket carries `AddTracingPolicy`, `DeleteTracingPolicy` and `ConfigureTracingPolicy` next to `GetEvents`; whoever can read events can also remove or disable the policies. The export filters do not apply to gRPC clients, so a reader gets the whole stream of every user's processes. sessiond is a user unit that reads only and is limited to `AF_UNIX` (SD17), and a user unit cannot hold a root-owned socket open. The socket therefore stays root-only, at `unix:///run/tetragon/tetragon.sock`, for `tetra` and the gate.
- **What gRPC would give, stated.** Server-side filters, no write to disk, and streaming without polling. It would cost a protobuf and gRPC dependency in the relay, the control-plane access above, and the loss of events while the relay restarts. The file keeps events across a relay restart and gives the administrator a record readable with `jq`.
- **The export settings** (drop-ins of TG2): `export-filename` `/var/log/tetragon/tetragon.log`; `export-allowlist` with `{"event_set":["PROCESS_KPROBE"],"policy_names":[...]}` naming our five policies, so exec and exit events of every process are not written (`event_set` takes the types that events.md documents, and all five policies are kprobes, so there is no `PROCESS_LSM`); field filters that drop `process.arguments`, `process.environment_variables`, `process.cwd` and their `parent.` counterparts; `enable-process-ns` true (TG3's classification); `enable-process-cred` true (TG5's); `export-file-max-size-mb` 10, `export-file-max-backups` 3, `export-file-compress` false so the relay can finish reading a rotated file; `export-rate-limit` per TG8. The `rate_limit_info` records are not filtered by the allow-list (section 1), so they reach the file.
- **Access to the file** _(Proposal)._ `/var/log/tetragon` is created by `systemd-tmpfiles` as `root:athanor-runtime-events`, mode 2750, so files created in it take the group; `export-file-perm` is `640`. The relay runs as the system user `athanor-runtime-events` with no capability, and that user is the only member of the group: no human account is. Whether the rotation keeps the group and mode is spike S6.
- **The relay** (`system/athanor-runtime-events`, Rust, `zbus`). It follows the file by offset and inode across rotations, keeps only events whose policy name begins with `athanor-` and the `rate_limit_info` records (they have no policy name; the exporter's limiter writes them past the filters), classifies the policy events (TG3, TG4, TG5, TG6), turns each `rate_limit_info` into one record of the category `events-dropped` with its count and no uid, attributes each policy event to a uid (the process's `uid`, else its `auid`), and keeps per uid a ring of the last 200 records in memory. A record holds the time, a category code from a closed list (`persistence-write`, `exec-temporary`, `credential-raise`, `module-request`, `events-dropped`), the binary path and the target path, and nothing else.
- **Its interface,** `os.athanor.RuntimeEvents1` on the system bus, built so that one user learns nothing of another's events (the one machine-wide record, `events-dropped`, is below).
  - `Subscribe()`, no argument. The relay takes the caller's uid from the bus daemon, never from an argument: `org.freedesktop.DBus.GetConnectionCredentials` on the sender's unique name returns `UnixUserID`, which the broker recorded from the kernel's peer credentials when the connection was made ([D-Bus specification](https://dbus.freedesktop.org/doc/dbus-specification.html#bus-messages-get-connection-credentials)). It stores the pair (unique name, uid) and forgets it when `NameOwnerChanged` reports the name gone.
  - A signal `Changed()`, with no payload, **unicast**: the relay sets the destination to the unique name of each subscriber of the uid concerned, so the broker delivers it to those connections only and no other user's connection sees it. It is never broadcast.
  - A method `Recent(cursor) -> (records, cursor)` that returns only the records of the caller's uid, the uid taken as above. The cursor is a per-uid counter, starting at 1 for each uid's ring, so its values say nothing about other users' volume. The records of `events-dropped` (no uid, no content) are served to every uid.
  - The bus policy lets only `athanor-runtime-events` own the name and lets every user call `Subscribe` and `Recent`. Events of uid 0 and of system users are not served to any session at 1.0 (Q5).
- **The relay's unit** passes TM8 with an empty capability set: `User=athanor-runtime-events`, `CapabilityBoundingSet=`, `NoNewPrivileges=yes`, `ProtectSystem=strict`, `ProtectHome=yes`, `PrivateNetwork=yes`, `RestrictAddressFamilies=AF_UNIX`, read-only access to `/var/log/tetragon`.
- **sessiond** calls `Subscribe()` once, calls `Recent` at start and again on each `Changed` it receives, and raises the notice (TG8), writing a journal record with a fixed `MESSAGE_ID` for each notice it posts (the gate reads it, section 5). It needs no new filesystem access, so SD17's "reads only" holds.

**TG8. Notices, the shield's sheet, and limits.**

- **Notices** _(Proposal)_ through `org.freedesktop.Notifications`, as SD12 does: summary from our own translations by category ("An application wrote to a startup location", "A program ran from a temporary folder", "A program tried to gain administrator rights", "A kernel module was requested", "Some security events could not be recorded"); body the binary's and the target's paths as plain text with control and bidirectional characters stripped (SH12); one notice per category per 10 minutes per session, a later one saying how many events it covers. No action beyond dismissal at 1.0.
- **The shield's sheet** gains a row "Security events", the count in the last 24 hours, opening the list of `Recent` records. The row never moves the badge: SH12 binds the badge to image verification, which these events are not. This is a change to `doc_bar.md` BR6 (section 3).
- **Volume limits, in layers** _(Proposal)_.
  - **Per policy, with an explicit scope.** Every `Post` carries `rateLimit: "1m"` and `rateLimitScope: "global"`. The default scope is the thread, so a process that is new at every exec is never limited; `global` collapses events whose first 40 bytes of each inspected argument are equal, whoever caused them. The arguments of TG3 (a directory or a start-up file), TG5 (an integer or a capability set) and TG6 (a module name) come from a small set, so `global` bounds each of them at one event per distinct value per minute however many processes or users repeat it. TG4's argument is the binary's path, which the caller chooses: `global` collapses a loop on one path and not a loop over many. A second user executing the same path within the minute is collapsed into the first's event; that is accepted.
  - **The daemon's `export-rate-limit` is a safety valve and not a budget:** 6000 events per minute, ten times what the layers above admit in normal use (to be measured at step 6), so that no policy's normal use reaches it. Beyond it events are dropped and counted in a `rate_limit_info` record.
  - **Dropped events are shown.** The relay keeps `rate_limit_info` (TG7): the count becomes an `events-dropped` record "N events not recorded" in every session's list and a notice, one per 10 minutes. A flood is therefore visible and never silent. The count carries no uid and no content; it is served to every session because it says that the machine's record is incomplete, and it tells a user that someone did a lot, not who or what.
  - The relay's ring of 200 per uid and the notice limit above.
- **Residual risk, stated.** The limiter is one per daemon and drops without regard to policy or user. A user whose process executes many distinct paths under `/tmp` (TG4's argument is chosen by the caller) can produce more than 6000 distinct events a minute, and while it lasts any event, a TG5 escalation included, may be counted in `rate_limit_info` instead of recorded. That user can therefore blind TG5 for the length of the flood, but not silently: the dropped count and the notice appear, and the flood's first records are in the list. An escalation inside the flood is lost, not delayed. Tetragon 1.7.1 offers no budget per user or per policy at the export (section 1), so this design does not close the gap. Q9 asks whether to accept it at 1.0.
- Needs: SD1, SD12.

**TG9. Privacy.** Events stay on the machine, consistent with `doc_kernel_profile.md` D34 and maintainer decision A2-19 (#161).

- The metrics server, the gops server and the health server are off: `metrics-server`, `gops-address` and `health-server-address` set empty. Tetragon opens no TCP or UDP listener (TG10).
- What is kept: the export file and its backups, at most 40 MB, readable by root and the relay; the relay's rings, in memory only, lost at reboot. The command line, the environment and the working directory of a process are never written (TG7's field filters).
- A user sees only their own events. Nothing is sent anywhere. A user who wants to include events in "Report a problem" copies them from the sheet; no automatic attachment.

**TG10. The service unit.**

- **Enabled by preset.** The package ships `/usr/lib/systemd/system-preset/80-athanor-tetragon.preset` with `enable tetragon.service`. Presets are read in lexical order of their file names and the first line that matches a unit wins, so this line is read before Fedora's `99-default-disable.preset` and its `disable *`. `systemctl enable tetragon.service` leaves `system/Containerfile:181`: `preset-all`, run in the same `RUN` and again in the NVIDIA stages, resets every unit to its preset, and with no preset line the result was "disabled" whatever was enabled before it. The image build's `preset-all` is what applies it; the package scriptlet is not relied on, because in a container build systemd is not running and `systemctl preset` does nothing. The image build asserts `systemctl is-enabled tetragon.service` after the last `preset-all`.
- **Capabilities** _(Proposal; the minimum is measured at step 1, since upstream publishes none)._ `CapabilityBoundingSet=` and `AmbientCapabilities=` `CAP_BPF CAP_PERFMON CAP_SYS_ADMIN CAP_SYS_PTRACE CAP_DAC_READ_SEARCH CAP_SYS_RESOURCE`. From the kernel's rules, unverified for Tetragon: `CAP_BPF` and `CAP_PERFMON` load tracing and LSM programs; `CAP_SYS_ADMIN` mounts the BPF and trace filesystems Tetragon checks at start (`main.go`: `CheckOrMountFS`, `CheckOrMountTraceFS`, `CheckOrMountCgroup2`) and reads other processes' namespace links; `CAP_SYS_PTRACE` and `CAP_DAC_READ_SEARCH` read `/proc/<pid>` of every process at start; `CAP_SYS_RESOURCE` covers the locked-memory limit on paths that ignore `LimitMEMLOCK=`. Dropped: `CAP_NET_ADMIN`, `CAP_SETUID`, `CAP_SETGID`, `CAP_DAC_OVERRIDE`, none of which our policies or settings use.
- **Sandbox.** Kept: `NoNewPrivileges=yes`, `ProtectSystem=strict`, `ProtectHome=yes`, `PrivateTmp=yes`, `ProtectKernelModules=yes`, `ProtectKernelLogs=yes`, `ProtectControlGroups=yes`, `LockPersonality=yes`, `RestrictSUIDSGID=yes`, `RestrictRealtime=yes`, `MemoryDenyWriteExecute=yes`, `LimitMEMLOCK=infinity`, `MemoryHigh=512M`, `MemoryMax=1024M`. Added: `StateDirectory=tetragon` (`/var/lib/tetragon`), `RuntimeDirectory=tetragon` (`/run/tetragon`, the socket), `ReadWritePaths=/sys/fs/bpf /var/log/tetragon` (`ProtectKernelTunables=yes` makes `/sys` read-only, and Tetragon pins its maps under `/sys/fs/bpf/tetragon`), `RestrictAddressFamilies=AF_UNIX AF_NETLINK` (unverified that nothing else is needed). Whether `ProtectKernelTunables=yes` also blocks a write Tetragon needs under `/sys/kernel/tracing` is part of step 1's measurement. `/usr/lib/tetragon` (`bpf-lib`, TG2) stays read-only: the objects are read, not written.
- **Ordering and restart.** `After=local-fs.target` without `network.target`, `WantedBy=multi-user.target`, `Restart=on-failure`, `StartLimitIntervalSec=2min` and `StartLimitBurst=10` as upstream's unit, `KillMode=control-group`.
- **No network listener.** `server-address` `unix:///run/tetragon/tetragon.sock`; `health-server-address`, `metrics-server` and `gops-address` empty.
- The unit passes TM8 through its allow-list. `scripts/verify.py` has no `services` check on this branch, so the check is a unit test of the repository (acceptance item 5) and a probe of the running unit (item 6).

**TG11. SELinux.** The image runs SELinux enforcing with the `targeted` policy (`doc_kernel_profile.md` D20 and D44). What Fedora's policy says about Tetragon, read on the maintainer's desktop on 2026-10-06 (`selinux-policy-targeted` 43.10-1.fc43 and `matchpathcon`; SELinux is disabled on that machine, so these are the installed policy's contexts and not a running system's):

- **There is no Tetragon domain.** No module and no file context names it: `/usr/bin/tetragon` is `bin_t`, `/usr/lib/tetragon` `lib_t`, `/var/log/tetragon` `var_log_t`, `/var/lib/tetragon` `var_lib_t`, `/run/tetragon` `var_run_t`, `/sys/fs/bpf` `bpf_t`. A service whose binary is `bin_t` is started by systemd in `unconfined_service_t` (the policy's default for services with no domain of their own; to be confirmed with `ps -eZ` at step 1, since the unit has never run on the image). **At 1.0 `tetragon.service`, and `athanor-runtime-events.service` with it (its binary under `/usr/bin` is `bin_t` as well), run unconfined by SELinux.** Their confinement is the unit's: the capability set, `NoNewPrivileges=yes` and the filesystem sandbox (TG10). SELinux does not block the daemon's use of `bpf`, `perf_event` and `lockdown`, so there is nothing to allow at first boot; for the same reason it gives no protection if the daemon is compromised.
- **The labels of the export file.** The daemon creates the file in `/var/log/tetragon`, whose default context is `var_log_t`, and the file inherits it. The relay, also `unconfined_service_t`, reads it by DAC alone: the group `athanor-runtime-events` and mode 0640 (TG7). No SELinux rule stands between them, and none stands between the file and a user: DAC alone refuses the user, who has neither the group nor the other bits.
- **A confined domain is the better end state and is not specified here.** It would need a local module like the ones the image already ships (`athanor_scx.cil`, `athanor_nix_daemon.cil` and `athanor_nvidia_modules_load.cil` in `/usr/share/selinux/packages` on the desktop): a type for each binary and for `/var/log/tetragon`, the permissions of the `bpf`, `perf_event` and `lockdown` classes, and a `dbus` `send_msg` rule for the relay's name. Q9. D20 already requires zero AVC denials in Athanor domains in acceptance; item 1 of section 5 checks it for these two units.

## 4. Spikes

1. **S1. The minimum capability set.** On the dev VM, start the unit of TG10 with each capability removed in turn; the set is the one where removing any capability makes the daemon fail to start, fail to load a policy, or miss an event of the gate. Also whether `ProtectKernelTunables=yes` and `RestrictAddressFamilies=` hold. Before step 1 merges.
2. **S2. The cost of TG3's `security_file_permission` rule.** The selector limits are documented (section 1) and a unit test enforces them (TG3), so they are no longer a spike. What is not known is the cost of a hook on every read and write of the machine. CPU time on the reference laptop under a build, with and without `athanor-persistence-write`; if it costs more than 1% of one core at idle, that policy is dropped and TG3 keeps only the creation hooks. Before step 2.
3. **S3. Attribution of a short-lived process.** Whether the relay can tell a Flatpak, a confined application and a toolbox apart from the event alone (the `docker` field, the mount namespace from `enable-process-ns`, the binary and its parent) when `/proc/<pid>` is gone. Before step 3.
4. **S4. `memfd` execution.** What path `linux_binprm` resolves for `fexecve` of a `memfd`, and whether a `Prefix` `/memfd:` matches it. Informs Q3.
5. **S5. The cost of `matchCapabilityChanges` on `fd_install`.** CPU time on the reference laptop under a build, with and without the rule. If it costs more than 1% of one core at idle, TG5 keeps only its first part.
6. **S6. The export file's mode and group across a rotation.** Whether the rotated and the new file keep group `athanor-runtime-events` and mode 0640. If not, the relay runs as uid 0 with an empty capability set, which reads a root-owned 0600 file as its owner.
7. **S7. Kernel restrictions that could disable Tetragon.** Forced lockdown at `integrity` allows kprobes and kernel memory reads by BPF; a move to `confidentiality` would forbid both and stop every policy (from the kernel's lockdown reasons, unverified on Azoth). `kernel.kptr_restrict=2` (`doc_kernel_profile.md` D47) and Tetragon's start-up probes of available functions. Both are risks to test and not conclusions: `ProtectKernelLogs=yes` removes `CAP_SYSLOG` and may block the symbol resolution that `kptr_restrict=2` already restricts. Read on the VM at step 1.
8. **S8. Noise and classification on a real machine.** On the reference laptop, with the kernel filters of TG4 and TG5 on, run Flatpak applications (with and without `--filesystem=home`), `rpm-ostree` and `nix-daemon` operations, an Electron application, Steam, an AppImage, rootless podman, toolbox, `sudo`, `su` and `pkexec`. Record what reaches the export file and what the relay keeps; list every source that raises a notice and was not meant to. Also whether `matchCapabilities` at the `setuid` hooks sees the caller's own effective set, and whether the relay can compute a `capset` gain from the event's fields. Before step 3; the counts go into revision 2.

2. **CI** `sudo tetra --server-address unix:///run/tetragon/tetragon.sock tracingpolicy list` lists the five `athanor-` policies, each enabled.
3. **CI** The closing criterion of #153: the test account `collaudo`, from its serial login, copies `/usr/bin/true` to `/tmp/athanor-tg-probe` and runs it. 4. **CI** Sockets, as root in the guest: `ss -lxp` lists `/run/tetragon/tetragon.sock` held by `tetragon`; `ss -ltunp` lists no socket held by `tetragon` (`-x` is what shows unix sockets, which `-ltnup` does not); `stat -c '%U %G %a' /run/tetragon/tetragon.sock` prints `root root 660`.
5. **repo** `python3 -B -m unittest discover -s forge/specs/athanor-tetragon/tests` (new, step 1) parses `SOURCES/tetragon.service` and the relay's unit and fails unless each has `NoNewPrivileges=yes` and a `CapabilityBoundingSet=` equal to the allow-list of TG10 and TG7; the same directory holds the policy-limit test of TG3 (step 2). **How `forge/test/iso` runs the CI items (1 to 4 and 6 to 8).** The probe is a script, `/usr/local/bin/athanor-tetragon-probe`, written only by the test kickstart (`collaudo.ks`, in a `%post`), so the image carries no test hook; the same kickstart adds the `collaudo2` account. `console.py` gains a `TETRAGON_PROBE`, typed over the serial line after `SESSION_ALIVE` and before Settings, at the serial login of `collaudo`: that is how `SESSION_DIAGNOSTICS` already reaches the account's user manager, and the commands of items 3 and 7 run as that account. ## 6. Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Q1. Enforcement.** Should any policy kill or deny at 1.0? Recommendation: no. Every policy observes; after revision 2's noise measurement, TG4 could deny exec from `/tmp` for tier 2 only, through `lsmhooks` and the BPF LSM (the kprobe `Override` action needs `CONFIG_FUNCTION_ERROR_INJECTION`, which Azoth leaves off). An alternative to any Tetragon enforcement is mounting `/tmp` and `/dev/shm` with `noexec` (`doc_kernel_profile.md`).
2. **Q2. Notices for tier 1.** Should a program run from `/tmp` by the user's own shell raise a notice, or only record? Recommendation: notice, aggregated, off in developer mode (TG4). TM1 says Athanor does not defend the user from themselves, but the user may not know a program they ran did this.
3. **Q3. More temporary directories.** Include `/var/tmp/` and `/run/user/` in TG4's first selector (they are its third and fourth values; the `Prefix` list is then at its limit of four)? Recommendation: yes to both; `memfd` goes in the second selector after S4.
4. **Q4. Module loads.** Notice only for a request by a uid other than 0, or never? Recommendation: notice for an unprivileged request only.
5. **Q5. System events with no session.** Events of uid 0 and of system users (a root daemon gaining capabilities, a module autoload at boot): to the sessions of `wheel` members, or only to the journal and the export file? Recommendation: `wheel` members' sessions for TG5 only, after the noise measurement.
6. **Q6. `/etc/tetragon/`.** Ship nothing there (upstream reserves it for the administrator), or keep an empty `tetragon.tp.d` as a visible place for local policies? Recommendation: ship nothing; document the drop-in that points `tracing-policy-dir` at `/etc`.
7. **Q7. Policy names in the export allow-list.** Should the administrator's own policies also reach the relay? Recommendation: no at 1.0; the relay serves only `athanor-` policies, and an administrator reads the export file.
8. **Q8. The relay's name.** `athanor-runtime-events` and `os.athanor.RuntimeEvents1` follow `doc_naming.md`; confirm.
9. **Q9. SELinux confinement and the flood.** Two risks this revision states and does not close. (a) At 1.0 the daemon and the relay run as `unconfined_service_t` (TG11): is that accepted, or does a local module with their own domains ship with step 1 and 3? Recommendation: accept at 1.0, specify the module in revision 2 once the VM shows the denials a confined domain would meet. (b) A user can blind TG5 for the length of a flood of distinct `/tmp` executions, visibly (TG8). Recommendation: accept at 1.0; the alternative is to stop watching `/tmp` for tier 1, which Q2 already questions.
